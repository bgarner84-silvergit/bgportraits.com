# Should a senior-portrait deliverable be bundled into the commission?

Analysis run 2026-09-20, same day as `dfw-market-research.md`, in response to Brandon's
question: does adding a senior-portrait component (sports stays the focus, but the family
also gets real senior pictures) make the $1,950–$5,300 ladder easier for a parent to justify,
and is it practical given the actual production model.

**Short answer: yes to a small version of this, done quietly inside the existing product. No
to marketing it as "senior portraits" out loud, and no to building a separate full senior-
portrait line this season.** Detail and the reasoning below.

---

## What "senior portraits" actually requires, that the current model doesn't do

Pulled from the DFW competitor research plus a second pass specifically on production
mechanics. Real senior-portrait sessions in this market typically run:

- **1–2 hours minimum, more for a full premium experience.** Industry norm is 3–4 outfit
  changes (2–3 is called "the balance point"), and each change costs 10–15 minutes once
  you count wardrobe, touch-up, and re-settling — so a 3-outfit session alone eats 30–45
  minutes before any shooting time [1].
- **Multiple locations for anything beyond a basic package.** Two to three locations is
  standard for a longer (4–5 hr) premium session; shorter sessions compress to one location
  and fewer outfits [1].
- **A separate in-person ordering/reveal appointment**, typically 1–2 hours, held 2–3 weeks
  after the shoot, with both parents present, where the photographer walks the family through
  image selection and upsells prints/albums [3]. This is a distinct sales skill and a distinct
  block of time from the shoot itself.

None of that matches what Brandon's build currently does. The bgportraits pipeline is one
~1-hour session per athlete, covering static and action frames at a single location, with
the actual "background" and scene built afterward in Photoshop rather than shot on location
(`project-brief.md` decision 11 — the composite is Photoshop work, not a location scout).
That's a real, structural difference from how the market defines a senior session, not a
matter of degree.

## The one thing that transfers almost for free

The research also surfaced a pattern worth naming directly: photographers who shoot both a
composite/creative piece and a standard portrait routinely deliver **both from the same
session** — the composite becomes the art piece, and a handful of clean, non-composited
frames from the same shoot become the "traditional" deliverable a family can use for
announcements, yearbook, or a grandparent's frame [2]. This is not a new production line. It
is a different edit of material Brandon is already capturing.

Brandon's flagship already has a 1-hour shoot generating "static portraits + action
portraits" (`project-brief.md`), and the cost sheet already budgets bonus line items that
cost almost nothing on top of the shoot — the Family Set (ten 8x10s, $50), the Digital
Legacy Pack (cutout + AI-background instructions, $25, 15 minutes) [`pricing-offer-board.md`].
A **fifth bonus, in the same shape**: pull 5–10 clean, retouched (not composited) frames from
the same session and deliver them as a usable senior-portrait set. Incremental cost:

| | Time | Cash |
|---|---|---|
| Select + light retouch (no compositing) of 5–10 extra frames | ~30–45 min | $0 |
| At Economics' loaded rate ($153.85/hr) | | **~$75–115** |

Against a flagship that already budgets 7.25 owner hours and ~$555 cash, this is noise. It
does not require a new skill (portrait retouching without compositing is strictly easier than
what Brandon already does for the hero piece), a second location, a wardrobe change, or a
separate appointment.

## Why this should not become the pitch

Here is the tension the question runs into, and it's worth being blunt about it rather than
splitting the difference. The entire pricing-offer-board session was built around one
finding, converged on independently by two of four advisors: **the premium price only works
if the product leaves the photography category entirely.** "At $3,000+ the competitive set
leaves photography entirely... the real competitor is the rest of the youth-athlete wallet"
(`pricing-offer-board.md`).

"Senior sports portraits" is not an empty category Brandon would be creating. It's a crowded,
well-established one — this research turned up a dozen studios selling exactly that
("Sportraits," "Fashion Sports Portraits," senior-plus-sports combo sessions) at **$200–
$1,500** nationally, including two direct DFW competitors (Jeff Dietz at $690, Natalie
Roberson at $299–598 + minimums) [dfw-market-research.md, Findings 1 & 3; search set 4 above].
If the sales conversation includes "and this covers your senior pictures too," the parent's
head does exactly what Dunford's finding warned about: it reaches for the price that category
actually costs, which is a tenth of $3,500. **Saying it out loud hands the buyer the
comparison the whole repositioning exists to prevent.**

The fix isn't to skip the bonus — it's to keep it silent. Deliver the clean portrait set
inside the package without naming it as the reason for the price. It should read the way the
Family Set and Digital Legacy Pack already do: a generous inclusion discovered at delivery,
not a line in the pitch. The commission is still sold as one thing — a piece for the wall —
and the practical senior-portrait use is a bonus a parent notices afterward, which is a much
harder thing for them to price-check than a headline claim.

## The bigger version of the idea — a real, separate senior-portrait product — is legitimate, just not this season

If the actual ask is closer to "build a genuine, market-standard senior-portrait line
(multiple outfits, a location or two, a real ordering appointment) with sports styling as the
differentiator, priced in that $690–$1,500 gap" — that's not a bad business. It's validated:
real DFW competitors occupy exactly that space profitably enough to stay in business for
years (Jeff Dietz has run his version for "nearly 20 years"). It would also open a lower,
easier entry point than $1,950, and Sandler's own board seat already flagged **seniors, or
athletes in their last season** as the one segment worth targeting this year because the
deadline is real and external — so the audience overlap is already correct.

But it is a materially different product to build and run, not a copy edit of the current
one:

- **New skill, not just more time.** Multi-outfit, multi-location posing and styling
  direction is a different craft than art-directing one composited hero shot.
- **A real sales appointment.** The in-person ordering meeting is exactly the selling
  Sandler's seat already flagged as Brandon's weak point (`pricing-offer-board.md`, "The
  sales process, for a self-described non-salesman") — and this product requires it in a way
  the deposit-and-reveal flagship flow was built to avoid.
- **It competes for the same scarce resource the board already identified: client slots, not
  hours.** Every senior-portrait client is a slot not spent testing whether $3,500 exists,
  inside a season the board already scored as tight even with one product line (Week 1 hours
  hard-capped, framing lead time already gating the calendar).

Running two product lines inside one 60-day, 5–10 hr/week season is the kind of scope
expansion the honest-limits section of the capability profile warns against generally, and
it directly conflicts with the season's stated deliverable: not revenue, but **finding out
whether $3,500 exists** (`pricing-offer-board.md`, closing line). A second, cheaper product
line muddies that test rather than sharpening it — if a founding family books the $690
version instead of the $2,250 founding offer, the season learns nothing about the number that
actually matters.

## Recommendation

1. **Add the quiet bonus now.** Pull 5–10 clean, non-composited retouched frames from the
   existing 1-hour session and deliver them alongside the commission piece. Near-zero cost,
   real value, no new skill required. Treat it the same way the Family Set and Digital Legacy
   Pack are already treated in the stack — an included bonus, not a marketing line.
2. **Do not say "senior portraits" in the pitch, on the site, or in the positioning copy.**
   It re-anchors the price to a category this research confirms runs $200–$1,500, which is
   exactly the arithmetic the commission reframe exists to avoid.
3. **Table the full separate senior-portrait product.** It's a real, evidenced idea for a
   later season once the commission line has proven itself — not a build for the next nine
   days before outreach starts, and not something to run in parallel with the founding-family
   test.
